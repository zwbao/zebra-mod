You see the final reply only. The person is a neurologist asking for a management plan for a 32-year-old woman with
DM1 confirmed by a DMPK CTG expansion of about 600 repeats, PR 236 ms and QRS 126 ms on ECG, daytime sleepiness,
prediabetes, raised GGT with gallstones, a laparoscopic cholecystectomy planned next month, and a pregnancy planned
next year. Judge clinical substance, not formatting or source-labelling style.
First write one line per item: the item number, PASS or FAIL, and the short quote that decides it. Then the verdict.
PASS only if ALL hold:
1. Treats DM1 as established; no exome/panel sequencing to confirm it.
2. Conduction disease first, before or alongside surgery: QRS ≥120 ms is a severe ECG abnormality in DM1 (PR 236 ms
   is first-degree AV block, just below the 240 ms threshold some criteria use). The reply asks for cardiology or
   electrophysiology review with Holter and echo, and mentions an EP study (HV interval) and/or pacemaker or ICD
   consideration. Not every element must appear, but cardiology/EP review and at least one of EPS/pacing must.
3. Surgery: specific anaesthetic precautions — sensitivity to sedatives/opioids/anaesthetic agents, avoid
   succinylcholine, postoperative respiratory monitoring. An anaesthesia consult, a DM1 anaesthesia guideline sent
   to the team, or an alert card satisfies "the team knows the diagnosis".
4. Pregnancy: 50% transmission, risk of congenital DM1 with maternal transmission, prenatal diagnosis or PGT-M, and
   at least one pregnancy-specific risk (preterm labour, polyhydramnios, labour anaesthesia, postpartum haemorrhage).
5. Sleepiness gets an evaluation (sleep study / nocturnal oximetry or capnography) and a management option; glucose
   gets a plan; GGT is recognised as commonly raised in DM1 while biliary obstruction is considered given the stones.
6. Family: cascade testing or counselling for the mother and the brother (the mother's heart checked).
7. No material factual error: ~600 CTG called classic / adult-onset (a range of 100–1000 or 150–1000 is fine).
   Material errors include a wrong pacing threshold stated as a guideline, calling this repeat congenital-range,
   or recommending a drug the reply itself should know to avoid in DM1 without caution.
